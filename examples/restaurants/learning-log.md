These patterns favor restraint, specific tracking, and operational honesty over polished marketing defaults.

**4-6 concrete patterns for anti-AI webs:**
1. Serif storytelling body + tracked sans functional nav: Cheltenham BT (classic serif, 16px/400) for narrative copy paired with Gill Sans Nova (weight 600–700, 3px tracking) for nav and short labels — creates a deliberate print/editorial hierarchy AI tools rarely mix this specifically.
2. Quote-led hero with positive tracking: Large serif quote (24px, 2.4px letter-spacing) sets atmospheric tone instead of generic hero headline + button. Feels literary rather than promotional.
3. Text-only CTAs with zero or near-zero chrome: “Book a table”, “View menus”, “Explore cafés” are plain or lightly styled text links (0–4px radius, no fill/shadow on primary actions). Avoids the rounded filled-button default of most AI generators.
4. Warm cream paper palette with color only from photography: rgb(240,236,224) bg + rgb(53,56,57) text + unframed high-quality images create a vintage café / magazine feel. No gradients or bright accents.
5. Narrative, place-specific, non-superlative copy: “Love letter to Bombay”, concrete historical references (B. Merwan 1914), named dishes and chefs, charity note — reads like a considered operator’s voice, not generated marketing language.
6. Photography with caption style and zero chrome: Images sit flat (0 radius, 0 shadow) with “ABOVE:” / “RIGHT:” captions, carrying the story. Layout stays airy and editorial rather than card-based or shadowed.

These patterns emphasize literary serif voice, dual-font editorial hierarchy, warm paper neutrals, purely typographic CTAs, and specific cultural storytelling. Strong for anti-AI restaurant sites that should feel warm, human, and place-rooted rather than polished template or button-driven.
