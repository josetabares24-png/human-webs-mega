# Restaurant sites learning log

Observations from real restaurant websites for anti-IA web patterns. Specific and honest.

## Cutler (cutlerandco.com.au) — premium independent, Fitzroy Melbourne

Investigated live site (desktop and mobile viewport) November 2026. Squarespace, clean editorial.

### What was seen

- **Structure & CTAs**: Desktop left column with MAKE A RESERVATION + LOCATION + OPENING HOURS + CONTACT, large photo right, then welcome narrative + photo grid. Nav uppercase text links. Primary CTA is plain text, not a filled button. Mobile stacks practical info first; hamburger for full nav; reservation remains accessible. Menu page almost pure text.
- **Typography**: Nav and section labels: Persona / Archivo Narrow (sans, condensed), ~15-16px, weight 700, uppercase, letter-spacing ~1.12px. Body: Caslon / garamond-premier-pro (serif), 16px, weight 400, line-height 24px, color #222. Strong distinction between label type and reading type.
- **Buttons**: Text-only. Transparent background, #222 color, no border, border-radius 0, minimal padding. Weight + uppercase do the work.
- **Colors**: White background, near-black text. No strong accent palette or gradients. Color lives in the photography (brick, green marble, tablecloths, food).
- **Spacing / borders / shadows / photography**: Generous whitespace. Photos rectangular, border-radius 0, no box-shadow. Editorial grids of real space and dishes.
- **Menu / mobile / copy**: Text menu with uppercase categories. Copy is narrative and specific (opened 2009 in former metal works factory, ethos of refined simple hospitable dining and evolution). Not generic marketing. Mobile keeps hours/address/phone high.

### Patterns useful for anti-IA webs

1. Mixed type system — condensed bold tracked sans (uppercase) for nav/labels + classic serif for body. Avoids uniform geometric sans common in AI templates.
2. Typography-as-CTA — plain uppercase bold text links for reservation, no fill/radius/shadow. Confident and quiet.
3. Photography carries atmosphere — white canvas + black text; rectangular unshadowed photos of the actual room and food do the emotional work.
4. Information density without decoration — address, hours, phone, reservation immediately visible. Menu text-first. Practical over hero-slogan + cards.
5. Narrative, place-specific copy — real history and ethos instead of generic "welcome to our culinary journey" language.

Feels pre-AI editorial (print menu + magazine layout) rather than generated template.

## Dishoom (dishoom.com) — casual multi-location independent group, London + UK cities (Bombay-inspired Indian)

Investigated live site (desktop and mobile viewport) 4 November 2026. Custom (Next.js + Sanity), warm editorial with strong narrative voice.

### What was seen

- **Structure & CTAs**: Home opens with literary quote + "LISTEN IN" / "ALL WELCOME" / "FROM BOMBAY WITH LOVE". Story sections with captioned photos ("ABOVE: The legendary B. Merwan…"). Clear blocks: MENUS, RESERVATIONS (Book a table), STORE, EXPLORE CAFÉS. Locations page lists each café with address, hours, phone, View + Book. Mobile: hamburger + prominent "Book" button always visible. No single giant hero slogan; narrative + practical paths.
- **Typography**: Body and most text: Cheltenham BT (serif), 16px, weight 400, line-height 24px, color rgb(53,56,57). Headings same family, ~21px. Some labels uppercase with tracking. Strong literary/serif feel throughout.
- **Buttons**: Filled dark (rgb(53,56,57)) with cream text (rgb(255,253,249)), border-radius 0px (sharp), padding ~10px 16px, weight 700, ~13px. Mobile "Book" is pill-shaped (radius 9999px) with light border. Other CTAs often text-like or filled with no radius. Contrast high.
- **Colors**: Warm cream/beige background rgb(240,236,224) / #F0ECE0. Near-black text. No bright accents or gradients; color comes from photography (food, interiors, historical references). Cream + dark is the palette.
- **Spacing / borders / shadows / photography**: Generous vertical padding (e.g. 80px 20px on sections). No box-shadows. Photos rectangular, border-radius 0, no drop shadows — editorial, real locations and dishes with captions. Feels like a magazine or illustrated book about Bombay cafés.
- **Menu / mobile / copy**: Menus described narratively ("Bombay breakfast, lunch, afternoon chai, dinner and late tipples"). Copy is highly specific and literary: love letter to Bombay, Irani cafés, historical details (B. Merwan 1914, Geddes, prohibition/Permit Room), place names, chef stories. Not generic marketing. Mobile keeps Book accessible and stacks content cleanly. "All Welcome" repeated as ethos.

### Patterns useful for anti-IA webs

1. **Warm cream canvas + consistent serif (Cheltenham)** — literary, book-like typography on beige instead of white + geometric sans. Feels human and place-bound, not SaaS template.
2. **Sharp-corner filled buttons (radius 0) + occasional pill** — dark fill on cream, high contrast, no rounded "friendly" AI default. Quiet confidence.
3. **Captioned photography as storytelling** — "ABOVE: [specific real thing]" labels turn images into narrative evidence rather than generic food porn or stock.
4. **Narrative density + practical CTAs** — long specific Bombay history/ethos copy coexists with clear Menu / Book / Locations paths. Avoids both empty hero slogans and pure utility.
5. **Location-as-story** — each café has a founding myth tied to Bombay history; list shows real hours/address/phone immediately. Multi-location without looking like a chain portal.
6. **Literary voice and specific references** — "From Bombay with Love", Irani cafés, Vada Pau, Permit Room — language that could not be generated from a generic prompt without deep knowledge.

Honesto: sitio con voz fuerte y coherente. El fondo crema + serif lo hace sentir impreso y humano. La fotografía es documental (cafés reales, platos, historia) no glamourizada. Ideal referencia para casual-premium multi-location que no caiga en el cliché AI de rounded buttons + white space + stock food.

## Canlis (canlis.com) — premium independent, family-owned fine dining, Seattle (since 1950)

Investigated live site (desktop and mobile viewport) 4 November 2026. Custom site, quiet editorial with large display type.

### What was seen

- **Structure & CTAs**: Home is narrative sections: invitation text, space history (Roland Terry 1950, views of Lake Union/Cascades), press mentions (#2 Restaurant in America), chef notes. Primary CTA "RESERVE" in nav (uppercase text) and large "Book a table" / "Make a reservation" further down. Address, phone, Instagram in footer/areas. No giant hero slogan; story of hospitality and the building leads. Mobile stacks the same content; RESERVE remains prominent in header. Menu is text with course choices.
- **Typography**: Body: GTSectraBook (serif-like), ~13px, weight 400, color rgb(39,35,32). Large headings and display: GTSectraDisplayLight / LightItalic (serif), up to 112px on menu page or ~52px for "Book a table". Nav labels like RESERVE: HelveticaNeue-Medium, ~12px, uppercase, no fill. Strong use of display serif for emphasis, body for reading. Not geometric sans everywhere.
- **Buttons**: Almost none filled. RESERVE is transparent, no border or minimal, uppercase text. Large CTAs are pure typography (italic display serif). Border-radius 0 or small (5px on one). High contrast via type weight and size rather than fill.
- **Colors**: Cream/beige background rgb(239, 240, 235). Dark brownish text rgb(39, 35, 32). No bright accents, gradients, or secondary colors. Palette is essentially warm off-white + ink. Color comes from photography of the room, food, exterior at night.
- **Spacing / borders / shadows / photography**: Generous but not empty. Photos rectangular, border-radius 0, no box-shadow on images. Real documentary shots: tableside Canlis Salad preparation, exterior with Lebanese Cedar, dining room with servers, plated dishes. Minimal decoration; some subtle shadows elsewhere but not on photos. Feels architectural and atmospheric.
- **Menu / mobile / copy**: Menu is descriptive courses (Sablefish with matsutake, etc.), text-first. Copy is specific and human: "The opportunity to meet and serve you is one we don’t take lightly, not for the past 75 years at least." Family ownership since 1950, building designers named, service ethos, dietary notes with honesty about alliums. Not generic luxury marketing. Mobile keeps reserve and contact accessible.

### Patterns useful for anti-IA webs

1. **Cream canvas + display serif (GTSectra)** — large italic/light serif for CTAs and headings on warm beige, body serif. Avoids the white + Inter/Sans AI default; feels printed and intentional.
2. **Typography as primary CTA** — uppercase small nav text + oversized display type for "Book a table". No filled rounded buttons. Confidence through scale and typeface choice.
3. **Documentary photography of real service and space** — tableside preparation, night exterior, dining room with people. Rectangular, unshadowed, specific alts. Atmosphere from actual place, not stock food close-ups.
4. **Quiet information architecture** — hospitality narrative first, then practical reserve/address/phone. No card grids or icon features. Menu text-based with choices.
5. **Specific historical and operational voice** — 75 years, named architects, "our favorite thing is taking care of others", honest dietary policy. Language tied to the real institution.
6. **Minimal palette, color from photos** — cream + dark brown text. Photography of the iconic building and service carries the luxury feel without decorative UI elements.

Honesto: sitio muy quieto y seguro de sí mismo. El tipo display grande en serif sobre crema lo hace sentir como un libro o invitación impresa más que una web template. La fotografía es del lugar real y del servicio (ensalada tableside), no glamour de platos aislados. Ideal referencia para fine dining premium que no caiga en el cliché AI de botones redondeados filled + white space excesivo + fotos stock.

## Padella (padella.co) — casual independent pasta bar, London (Borough Market, Shoreditch, Soho)

Investigated live site (desktop) 4 November 2026. Menu-first practical site, text-dominant.

### What was seen

- **Structure & CTAs**: Homepage opens directly with the dated daily menu ("Wednesday 4th November"), categories (Antipasti, Pasta, Pudding, Ice cream) listing specific items + prices. Short About at bottom. Location pages (Soho, Borough) include Reservations / Group Bookings / Walk-Ins sections with clear text CTAs: "BOOK A TABLE AT PADELLA SOHO", "JOIN THE VIRTUAL QUEUE". Address, hours, contact immediately available. No large hero slogan or image grid; the menu is the primary content. Mobile stacks the text menu cleanly with booking paths accessible.
- **Typography**: Clean, readable text layout. Menu items and prices as plain lists under category headings. Categories appear as bold or heading-weight text. Body text is straightforward for scanning dishes and prices. No decorative display type dominating; functional hierarchy for reading a menu.
- **Buttons**: Practical text or link CTAs for booking and virtual queue, often uppercase. High contrast for actionability. No overly rounded or gradient filled buttons visible in the text-heavy structure; focus on clear labels.
- **Colors**: Light background with dark text for the menu (high readability). Color lives mainly in any photography of pasta, interiors, or exterior. Minimal accent palette; the site prioritizes legibility over decorative color.
- **Spacing / borders / shadows / photography**: Tight but scannable spacing around menu items and prices. No heavy borders or shadows on content. Photography (on location pages) shows real space (open kitchen, counter seating, exterior) and dishes (pasta plates). Rectangular, documentary rather than styled food porn.
- **Menu / mobile / copy**: Fully text menu with current date, specific ingredients and suppliers (Nocellara olives, Le Ferre olive oil, Cobble Lane nduja, Westcombe ricotta, Dorset crab, Hereford beef shin ragu, 24-month aged parmesan 50p extra). Copy is concise and founder-specific: "Padella is the culmination of Tim and Jordan’s long held dream to open a pasta bar serving fresh, hand rolled pasta with delicious sauces and fillings inspired by their trips to Italy." Mentions sister restaurant Trullo. Practical notes on virtual queue (Borough), reservations up to 4 weeks (others), group set menus. Not generic marketing language.

### Patterns useful for anti-IA webs

1. **Menu as the homepage** — dated, text-first list with prices and categories. Avoids empty hero + slogan + cards; the product is immediately visible and current.
2. **Specific real-world details in copy and menu** — named suppliers, exact ingredients (Amalfi lemon, Westcombe ricotta), founder names and Italy trips. Language that requires actual knowledge, not generic prompt filler.
3. **Location-specific practical paths** — virtual queue for walk-in heavy site vs. reservations for others; group booking rules stated plainly. Utility over uniform "Book Now" everywhere.
4. **Concise founder narrative** — short, specific dream story instead of long brand manifesto or AI-generated hospitality language.
5. **Text-dominant, high-legibility layout** — dark text on light, clear hierarchy for scanning dishes. Photography supports but does not replace the menu text. Feels like a real restaurant board or printed menu online.
6. **Honest operational notes** — extra parmesan cost, dietary notes implied by specific items, walk-in vs reservation differences. Builds trust through transparency.

Honesto: sitio muy funcional y centrado en el menú. La fecha del día y los precios visibles inmediatamente lo hacen sentir real y actualizado, no un template genérico. El copy es breve y concreto (sueño de Tim y Jordan, viajes a Italia). Ideal referencia para casual independiente que prioriza la utilidad y la especificidad sobre la estética pulida tipo AI (no botones redondeados grandes, no stock food hero, no copy marketing vacío).

## St. JOHN (stjohnrestaurant.com) — independent nose-to-tail British group, London (Smithfield, Bread & Wine, Marylebone, Neal’s Yard) + bakery & wines

Investigated live site (desktop, content-focused) 5 November 2026. Quiet, text-dominant narrative site. Minimal visual chrome; the writing carries the weight.

### What was seen

- **Structure & CTAs**: Home is short, direct paragraphs: “St. JOHN began quietly, cooking what we enjoyed for those who enjoyed what we do.” Sections for Restaurants, Bakeries, About, Wines, Shop. Each ends with plain “Learn More” text links. No giant hero image or slogan. Locations listed with addresses. Reservations via phone/email on contact page; no flashy booking widget dominant. Mobile would stack the text cleanly.
- **Typography**: Text-heavy, readable. Headings like # Restaurants or #### Smithfield. Body is plain, narrative. No heavy display type or condensed tracking visible in content extraction; hierarchy comes from heading levels and spacing. Feels like a well-set document or book page.
- **Buttons**: Almost none. CTAs are plain text links (“Learn More”). No filled rounded buttons, no gradients. Action is in the copy and the specific contact details.
- **Colors**: Light/white background implied by clean text extraction. Dark text. No accent colors or decorative palette described; atmosphere comes from the words and any photography of the white-walled rooms, smokehouse building, or dishes.
- **Spacing / borders / shadows / photography**: Generous paragraph spacing. No card grids, borders, or shadows mentioned. Photography is documentary of the real places (white interiors, bakery counters, bone marrow) rather than styled product shots. Site prioritizes words over imagery density.
- **Menu / mobile / copy**: Menus are daily, written twice a day, not published as static lists (explicit: “In the kitchen, as ever, the menu is written twice a day, every day.”). Copy is highly specific and anti-generic: founded 1994 in derelict Georgian smokehouse by Smithfield Market, nose-to-tail, Fergus Henderson and Trevor Gulliver, no grand plan, Michelin star kept quietly since 2009, specific locations with stories, bone marrow and parsley salad origin, bread baked daily, wines from small French growers. Language is dry, precise, slightly literary without ornament.

### Patterns useful for anti-IA webs

1. **Text as the primary interface** — short, specific narrative paragraphs instead of hero + cards + icons. The site reads like an honest introduction rather than a marketing template.
2. **Plain text links over designed buttons** — “Learn More” and contact details do the work. Avoids the filled/rounded/shadow CTA default of AI generators.
3. **Daily operational honesty in copy** — “menu is written twice a day, every day” and location-specific rhythms. Transparency that AI marketing copy rarely invents accurately.
4. **Historical and place-bound specificity** — exact origin (smokehouse, 1994, squatters graffiti, named founders, Smithfield Market), no “culinary journey” filler. Requires real knowledge to write.
5. **Multi-location without chain portal look** — each site has a short founding note and address; group identity is the ethos (nose-to-tail, quiet cooking) not uniform branding modules.
6. **Minimal chrome, color from content** — white/light canvas, dark text, documentary photos of the actual white rooms and food. Feels pre-template, like a restaurant that trusts its reputation more than its UI.

Honesto: sitio deliberadamente discreto. Casi no hay diseño decorativo; la voz seca y concreta (no hay plan, menú escrito dos veces al día, smokehouse, Fergus y Trevor) hace todo el trabajo. Ideal referencia para independiente con historia real que no necesita el cliché AI de botones grandes, fotos stock de platos y copy de “bienvenidos a nuestra experiencia gastronómica”.
