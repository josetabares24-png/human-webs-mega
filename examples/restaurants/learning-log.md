## Atomix (atomixnyc.com) — 2026-10-31
Premium independent Korean tasting-menu restaurant in NYC (Squarespace).

**Observed:**
- Structure: Minimal horizontal nav (COLLABS, CHEF'S COUNTER, BAR TASTING, ABOUT US). Hero is mostly photography + logo. Main CTAs are "BOOK NOW" and "CURRENT MENU". Sparse pages with short paragraphs and practical notes.
- Typography: Logo/headings use Ambroise Std (serif, ~19px, weight 400, letter-spacing 5.7px, uppercase). Body is Heebo (sans, 15px, weight 300, line-height 34.5px ≈ 2.3). Subheads sometimes Work Sans. High tracking on nav and buttons.
- Buttons: Sharp rectangles (border-radius 0), filled deep indigo rgb(33, 33, 192), white text, padding ~13px 26px, letter-spacing 3.5px, Heebo. No outlines or text-only primary CTAs. High contrast, editorial.
- Colors: Pure white bg rgb(255,255,255), near-black text rgb(26,26,26) or rgb(5,5,5). Accent is the indigo on buttons. No gradients, no secondary colors dominating.
- Spacing / borders / shadows / photography: Almost no box-shadows or borders. Images are large, high-quality professional food/interior shots (credited, Squarespace CDN), object-fit fill, no frames. Generous vertical rhythm from line-height; containers have light horizontal padding (~17px). Clean, airy.
- Menu / mobile / copy: Menu is linked externally or PDF-style. Dietary restrictions stated bluntly ("not able to accommodate vegan..."). Copy is restrained: "NEW KOREAN CUISINE", short origin story, practical email for wine list or inquiries. Awards listed factually (Michelin 2-stars, etc.). Mobile likely collapses nav (Squarespace default); desktop is clean horizontal.

**4-6 concrete patterns for anti-AI webs:**
1. Letter-spacing as luxury signal: 3.5–5.7px tracking on nav, logo, and CTAs creates deliberate, non-default rhythm that AI generators rarely apply consistently.
2. Weight contrast within one sans: body at 300 with 2.3 line-height vs buttons at regular weight + tracking — feels editorial, not system-ui default.
3. Sharp (0 radius) filled indigo buttons on white — avoids the rounded-pill / soft-shadow aesthetic that dominates AI sites.
4. Photography without chrome: large unbordered, unshadowed images carry the page; text is secondary and sparse. No stock-photo look or heavy overlays.
5. Honest, practical copy: explicit limitations (allergies, corkage $100, email for list) instead of marketing superlatives. Reads like a real operator, not a prompt.
6. Dual-font with custom serif for brand mark only: Ambroise Std used sparingly for logo/heading, Heebo everywhere else — restrained hierarchy instead of everything in one trendy sans.

These patterns favor restraint, specific tracking, and operational honesty over polished marketing defaults.

## Ma‘ono Fried Chicken (maonoseattle.com) — 2026-10-31
Independent casual Hawaiian-style fried chicken (multiple Seattle locations, Squarespace).

**Observed:**
- Structure and CTAs: Full-bleed bright yellow background. Top nav text links (Menu, Locations, About, Contact, FAQ) + orange Catering button. Hero: oversized “HAWAIIAN STYLE FRIED CHICKEN” + prominent “ORDER ONLINE”. Short location cards (West Seattle / Benbow Room, etc.). Repeating marquee “CRISPY ∗ JUICY ∗ ALOHA”. About page leads with pronunciation and personal family story.
- Typography: Display headings use custom “obviously-narrow” (Typekit, weight 800) at large sizes (~63px hero, ~45px marquee, ~28px section heads). Body is system sans-serif 16px / 400. Some buttons/nav use “degular” or “Varela Round”. Extreme weight and size contrast; dark navy text on yellow.
- Buttons: Primary CTAs (ORDER ONLINE, Catering, skip-to-content) are filled orange rgb(226, 75, 37), white text, modest border-radius ~6px, padding ~16px. Nav items are plain text links (no fill). High contrast against yellow.
- Colors: Dominant bright yellow bg rgb(254, 195, 59), dark navy text rgb(27, 36, 46), orange accent. Saturated, energetic palette; no gradients or muted tones.
- Spacing / borders / shadows / photography: Essentially no box-shadows or decorative borders. Squarespace food and location photos (some full-size). Marquee creates motion. Bold type provides visual rhythm more than spacing system.
- Menu / mobile / copy: On-site menu with photos and casual descriptions (e.g. “Your first dipping sauce is free but you should probably get more”). Locations list hours and practical notes (pick-up door, alley entrance). Copy is personal and specific (Kauai summers with grandparents, secret seasoning, King’s Hawaiian bun, “so ono”). Mobile shows hamburger “Open Menu”; desktop is open nav.

**4-6 concrete patterns for anti-AI webs:**
1. Full-bleed saturated brand color as page background (bright yellow instead of white/gray) — instantly personal and non-template.
2. Ultra-bold custom display face (obviously-narrow 800) at very large sizes for headlines, paired with plain body text — strong hierarchy without relying on default system or trendy sans.
3. Idiosyncratic repeating marquee with special characters (∗) — playful, human detail that AI generators rarely invent with consistent personality.
4. Cheeky, operational menu copy that includes practical asides (“first dipping sauce is free…”) — reads like a real kitchen, not marketing polish.
5. High-energy filled orange buttons with modest radius on yellow field — avoids both soft pastels and pure black/white defaults.
6. Hyper-specific personal origin story (named family members, exact place Kauai/Koke‘e, picnic chicken memory) — concrete detail over generic passion statements.

These patterns favor loud, specific personality and operational voice over restrained luxury or polished marketing defaults.

4. Ingredient-transparent menu copy with explicit allergen lists next to playful product names — operational honesty + personality that reads like a real kitchen, not AI-generated polish.
5. Product photography that is detailed and specific (hand-held wraps, ingredient-focused) without UI chrome, shadows, or frames — images do the selling; layout stays flat and clean.
6. Seasonal, idiosyncratic headlines ("The Caesar Got Dilly") over generic benefit statements — concrete, human voice that AI generators struggle to keep consistent and non-cliché.

These patterns favor brand-specific custom type, warm organic color, transparent operational details, and playful specificity over generic polished marketing or default system aesthetics.

## Cutler & Co. (cutlerandco.com.au) — 2026-10-31
Premium independent fine-dining restaurant in Fitzroy, Melbourne (Squarespace). Former metalworks factory; chef Andrew McConnell.

**Observed:**
- Structure and CTAs: Horizontal uppercase nav (Reservations, Menu, Drinks, Private Dining, Gift Vouchers, Journal, Sundays at Cutler). Hero area is mostly large photography of the dining room (plants, brick, tables) with logo. Practical sidebar-like blocks: MAKE A RESERVATION, LOCATION (address), OPENING HOURS, CONTACT (phone + email). CTAs are text links, not prominent filled buttons. Sparse, functional layout. Mobile has "Open Menu" hamburger.
- Typography: Nav and section heads use Persona + Archivo Narrow (sans, ~15-16px, weight 700, letter-spacing ~1.1–3px, uppercase). Body and menu items use Caslon / garamond-premier-pro (serif, 16px, weight 400). Logo is image-based. High tracking on nav; serif for readable menu text.
- Buttons: Almost no filled buttons. Primary actions are plain text links (0 radius, no fill, no border). "MAKE A RESERVATION" is uppercase text with modest tracking. Skip-to-content is dark filled rectangle. High contrast black/dark on white; editorial restraint.
- Colors: Pure white bg rgb(255,255,255), dark text rgb(34,34,34) or black. No strong accent colors, no gradients. Minimalist monochrome palette. Photography provides all color.
- Spacing / borders / shadows / photography: No box-shadows, no decorative borders, 0 border-radius everywhere. Large high-quality professional photos (interiors with brick/plants, plated dishes, wine cellar, people dining) from Squarespace CDN, unbordered, no overlays or frames. Generous white space; type hierarchy is subtle. Airy, calm, premium.
- Menu / mobile / copy: Full on-site text menu with prices and specific Australian produce (Corner Inlet calamari, Loddon Valley lamb, Yarra Valley salmon roe, Martins Bay whitebait). Categories like APPETISERS, CUTLER CLASSICS. Copy is factual and place-specific: origin in former metalworks factory 2009, ethos "refined, simple, hospitable", walk-in bar vs reserve dining room, acknowledgement of Wurundjeri Woi Wurrung Traditional Custodians. Hours and address prominent. No marketing fluff.

**4-6 concrete patterns for anti-AI webs:**
1. Text-link CTAs with heavy tracking and uppercase instead of filled pills or buttons — creates deliberate editorial restraint that avoids the default "rounded blue CTA" look AI sites love.
2. Serif body (Caslon/garamond) for menu items paired with condensed sans (Archivo Narrow) for heads — classic newspaper-like hierarchy that feels human and non-system-ui.
3. Practical operational blocks (hours, address, phone, email) sitting prominently next to photography — functional honesty over hero slogans or benefit claims.
4. Specific geographic and seasonal menu language (named bays, valleys, farms) with prices in plain text — reads like a real kitchen list, not generated descriptions.
5. Zero chrome on photography (no shadows, radii, or frames) + pure white canvas — images and white space do the work; layout stays flat and timeless.
6. Place-rooted origin story with concrete details (former metalworks factory, glass façade, Fitzroy) and cultural acknowledgement — specific local voice instead of generic "passionate about food" statements.

These patterns favor editorial typography, operational transparency, and photographic restraint over polished marketing defaults or trendy UI chrome.
